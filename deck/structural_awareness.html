02 · the problem

We choose architectures too early

Training a biological sequence model to convergence is expensive. So the field takes a shortcut: train each architecture briefly, measure loss (or a probe), and scale the apparent winner.

That shortcut assumes early rankings predict final biological capability.

One-page memo

Early biological probes can mis-rank sequence-model architectures

Summary

We train Transformer (GPT-2-style) and Mamba (state-space) language models on protein sequences (UniRef50) and DNA (human genome), save checkpoints throughout training, and probe the internal activations with a linear read-out for biological structure. The central finding is a training-time architecture rank-reversal: the state-space model exposes protein secondary structure and DNA reading frame earlier, but the Transformer overtakes by convergence. The language-model loss does not show the same reversal, so early loss-based architecture selection can choose the wrong model.

Why this matters

Biological foundation models are often selected using early training signals, because full training is expensive. Our results suggest that biological representation development is architecture-dependent: the model that looks best early may not have the strongest converged biological representation. An early proxy can therefore mis-rank architectures — and loss will not warn you.

Current evidence

On the corrected compute axis with a hierarchical posterior over 10 seeds, the reversal is supported for protein secondary structure (ss3/M P=0.92, ss8/M P=0.75) and replicates cross-domain on DNA reading frame (frame/M P=0.93), while GC composition is a clean negative control (gc P=0.00). A capability forecaster uses early trajectories to recover the converged architecture ranking under leave-one-cell-out evaluation; at a 10%-compute operating point it recovers the final ranking while saving roughly 90% of compute, and it is FLOP-invariant (byte-identical under the compute-accounting correction).

The two-paper program

Paper B (this pitch) establishes that early biological probes can mis-rank architectures and that a calibrated forecaster fixes the decision cheaply. Paper A develops the developmental dynamics of biological representation learning: when different biological concepts become decodable, how architecture changes that timeline, and whether matched-loss models expose different amounts of learned structure — captured by an inductive-bias coefficient γ > 0 (at equal loss, the Transformer exposes more decodable structure).

Feedback requested

We would especially value feedback on the biological framing, the single strongest validation experiment, and whether this is best positioned as an ICLR / MLCB-style ML paper or as part of a broader structural-biology representation-learning story.
